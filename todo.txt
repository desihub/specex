  3. JAX vs. NumPy/Numba/CuPy
  Regarding your supervisor's question on feasibility:
   * Feasibility: It is 100% feasible to reproduce these results with Numba/CuPy, but with a significant increase in development effort.
   * The "JAX Advantage": The primary reason we used JAX was Automatic Differentiation (AD). The Gauss-Hermite PSF model requires complex derivatives for 55+ parameters per spot. In JAX, this is one line of code (jax.grad). In Numba/CuPy, we would have to manually derive, code, and unit-test thousands of lines of
     math, effectively re-implementing the C++ manual derivative logic.
   * Performance: JAX's XLA compiler performs global optimizations (like kernel fusion) that are difficult to achieve manually in CuPy without writing custom CUDA C++ kernels.
   * Recommendation: JAX is now the standard for this type of "Differentiable Physics" problem. It is much more maintainable and less prone to human error in the derivative math.
